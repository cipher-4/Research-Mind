# Comprehensive Research Dossier: give me all the information about ethanol

## Executive Summary & Key Findings
Ethanol (CH3CH2OH) is a 2-carbon alcohol with a molecular mass of 46.041 g·mol−1, primarily produced through hydration or fermentation. It is a volatile, colorless liquid with a higher octane number than gasoline, making it a viable alternative fuel for internal combustion engines. As a fuel, ethanol enhances thermal efficiency and compression ratios, with blends like E60 achieving 34.5% thermal efficiency. While lower ethanol concentrations (e.g., E30) can slightly reduce indicated specific fuel consumption (ISFC) compared to pure gasoline (from 397 g/kWh to ~380 g/kWh), higher concentrations (E60, E100) lead to increased ISFC due to ethanol's lower heating value. A significant benefit of ethanol is its positive impact on vehicle emissions, with E60 demonstrating substantial reductions in CO2 (16%), CO (27%), NOx (38%), and unburned hydrocarbons (57%) compared to pure gasoline, attributed to its oxygen content and lower carbon per unit of energy.

Despite its fuel advantages, ethanol production faces considerable practical constraints. These include substantial land use requirements for feedstocks like corn and sweet sorghum (ethanol production accounts for 12% of total land use), and significant water demands, with a clear trade-off between irrigated and rainfed systems. Economic viability is highly sensitive to feedstock and ethanol pricing, often requiring improvements in recovery rates and favorable market prices (e.g., a 4.9% recovery rate and Rs 29/liter ethanol price for sweet sorghum to be attractive). Policy support is deemed crucial for the commercialization and broader adoption of various ethanol feedstocks, including cellulosic biomass. Overall, ethanol offers a cleaner-burning fuel with well-understood chemical properties, but its widespread implementation is challenged by resource-intensive production and economic sensitivities that necessitate ongoing research, technological advancements, and supportive policy frameworks.

*   **Abbreviation**: EtOH [1].
